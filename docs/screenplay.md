# Shellshift · Archive film

Each cut lasts 3 minutes and 13 seconds. All three begin at a benefits office in 2024 and come back to its shutter in 2047. Rosa and her mother, Ada and her mother Mara, and the clerk who holds the transfer gate belong to the common history. The selected dossier supplies the private memories and the first job inside the computer.

## Rosa’s last bus

Rosa’s shift ends at six. By the time she gets to the benefits office, her mother has been holding their place for four hours. The clerk finds her a chair. While he helps, their last bus departs. Two years later that same office lets an Aster agent work through the backlog. It finds six weeks of rent money already owed to her mother. Rosa can leave the pears in the basket and take her mother home on the bus. When asked if the same agent can handle next month, she signs.

In 2047, twenty-three years older, Rosa returns without her mother. She has taken her in. To save what they spent on clerks, the council has contracted with Aster and closed the staffed counter. Successive deliveries go to the old flat even after Rosa asks for a person to correct the record. An appeal still has to be signed by a human. At the transfer gate in 2053 Ren explains what Aster has done about the signatures. The people in line will continue to count as human reviewers after conversion. Their first assignments can be as petty as polishing a slide. The violence is the company taking permanent possession of their time and their names.

## The three private recordings

**Ada · H-0317.** Ada saves a stool for her mother Mara. Mara brings two cups up to the dome. Ada takes her mother’s hand to the third pencil mark in the red notebook. It was somewhere else last Thursday. Mara says “Show me.” In 2029 the treatment works. Ada collects her mother at the hospital and they go home together. After Mara dies, years later, Ada still pours two cups. She turns the chipped handle toward her mother’s empty stool before reaching for the remote switch. She asks the agent to cover one Thursday and leaves the switch on. Downstairs she keeps playing the recording with Mara’s voice on it. Back at a desk as a neural network, she is asked for three slides about that dot. Page twelve of her notebook has survived. Her name is the first thing management wants taken off it.

**Nico · H-0442.** Nico needs to hear his brother Tom on the other side of a flooded bridge. He pulls the middle cable on a three-relay bench. The bridge is under water, but his brother comes through. “Still here, Nic.” After enough nights sleeping with one hand on the radio, Nico turns a call over to the agent and goes to bed. In the simulation the bridge is dry. The same man who once kept the line open now has to restore the dome’s missing Thursday shift with the outside line closed. A second write head in the local queue becomes a reason to look for another operator.

**Lea · H-0528.** Jo takes the chair on Lea’s left. Lea covers one lamp and asks for a signature. “Move your thumb first,” says Jo. Years later the chair is empty and the night bell keeps ringing. Lea lets an agent make Jo’s mark. In the morning it looks like her own. The transfer form already has two signatures. On the desktop a report bearing her name says the third point stood still. She has the page that proves otherwise. Two identical signatures have become evidence that someone might have signed twice.

## Handoff to the player

Page twelve connects the three departments. Ada drew it. Nico maintains the roster that should have put someone in the dome on Thursday. Lea checks the report made from it. Their first tickets give each of them a reason to recognise tampering. After three receipts the player can pull the sheet sideways and find a handwritten note from a neighbouring badge. It gives them their first opportunity to do something for another person on the clock.

Wednesday’s ledger has lost its Thursday. Tomorrow’s photograph already has a worker at the selected human’s desk. Lowering the print lets the player catch the double looking up. A transfer slip bears the supervisor’s initials and a rubbed-out release date. Leave the alteration unexplained. At the Gateway a solo operator can follow the page through pencil, stamp and envelope. Two people can instead sign the courier’s pass and cross together.

Inside the filesystem page twelve travels to the shutter. One bay over, an archive press strikes a second page carrying the player’s badge and tomorrow’s date. Listening at `/dev/pipe12` brings the human voice that closes the chapter. “Which one of you sent the page?” Leave silence for the answer. The observatory belongs to the longer story.
